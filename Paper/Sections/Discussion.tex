\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Postgres IVM}
Though a powerful plugin, Postgres IVM has limitations in the forms of materialized views it can maintain incrementally. Postgres IVM cannot handle COUNT DISTINCT or GROUP BYs on an aggregation function as shown in \textcite{pg_ivm}. As a result of this, we are limited to the number of workloads that Postgres IVM supports. Additionally, the fact that the plugin updates materialized views via database triggers is likely an area that would require some performance optimization.

\subsection{Future Work}
Currently, our plugin supports selecting a subset of columns from a table and up to an 8-way join of existing tables. We currently lack support for any aggregation functions (though the workload may still see some successful increases if there is a join present). This would be a great avenue to increase support for future work. Additionally, the use of pg\_stat\_statement to analyze the differing execution time between the initial queries to the base tables versus the IMMV may be helpful.

A different avenue to pursue for general speedups could also be the addition of indexes to IMMV tables, or base tables. These indexes could also be selected in an automated way: they behave similarly to materialized views in that they require extra information on a write, but are automatically updated and wouldn't require the Postgres IVM plugin. However, they are also incapable of mapping a join.
